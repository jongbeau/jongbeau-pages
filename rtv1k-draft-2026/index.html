How the grades work

Every pick is weighed by what was at stake. The second overall pick had 181 points of value on the table; the last pick had 10.

Three boards, because they disagree in useful ways. Market ADP is a blend weighting FFC highest, since it comes from real ten-team PPR drafts. ECR is FantasyPros alone. VORP is projected points above a replacement starter. Where ADP and ECR sit more than forty places apart, the pessimistic reading is used.

Taking the best player left is its own credit. Early on there is no value to capture, so a pick that takes the top player on the board floors at A. That floor is suspended when the sources split, because it is computed from VORP, and VORP has no concept of a player being unable to play.

Bench counts what beats the waiver wire. Two earlier versions of this were wrong: summing every reserve punished depth, and scoring reserves below replacement as negative punished teams whose picks went into strong starters. A reserve worse than a freely available player is worth zero, because that is what you would do with him.

Draft slot is not skill. Slots 1–3 averaged 484 points of startable roster; slots 8–10 averaged 414. The grade controls for that; the starters column does not.

Grades measure decision quality against what was knowable on the night. Projection R² is about 0.24, so outcome noise is roughly twice the spread of the projections. None of this predicts the season.
