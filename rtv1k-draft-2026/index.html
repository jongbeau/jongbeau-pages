How the grades work

Every pick is weighed by what was at stake. The second overall pick had 181 points of value on the table; the last pick had 10. Grading them equally would say a kicker matters as much as a first-rounder.

Three boards, because they disagree in useful ways. Market ADP is a blend weighting FFC highest, since it comes from real ten-team PPR drafts. ECR is FantasyPros alone. VORP is projected points above a replacement starter. Where ADP and ECR sit more than forty places apart, the pessimistic reading is used: a gap that large almost always means news one source has absorbed and the other has not.

Taking the best player left is its own credit. Early on there is no value to capture, so a pick that takes the top player still on the board floors at A regardless of ADP. That floor is suspended when the sources are split, because it is computed from VORP, and VORP has no concept of a player being unable to play.

Roster need moves a grade one step. Filling an empty starting slot earns it; adding depth while a starting slot sits empty loses it.

Draft slot is not skill. Slots 1–3 averaged 484 points of startable roster; slots 8–10 averaged 414. The grade controls for that by judging each pick against what was actually available; the starters column does not.

Grades measure decision quality against what was knowable on the night. Projection R² is about 0.24 — outcome noise is roughly twice the spread of the projections — so none of this predicts the season.
