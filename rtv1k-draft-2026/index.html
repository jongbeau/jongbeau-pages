Two ways to read the same draft

Roster value asks how much talent a team ended up with. The draft grade asks how well each pick was made against what was still on the board. They are not the same question, and the teams that diverge most are the interesting ones.

How the grades work

Every pick is weighed by what was at stake. The second overall pick had 181 VORP on the table; the last pick had 10. Grading them equally would say a kicker matters as much as a first-rounder.

Three boards, because they disagree in useful ways. Market ADP is a blend weighting FFC highest, since it comes from real 10-team PPR drafts. ECR is FantasyPros alone. VORP is projected points above a replacement starter. Where ADP and ECR sit more than 40 places apart, the pessimistic reading is used: a gap that large almost always means news one source has absorbed and the other has not.

Taking the best player left is its own credit. Early on there is no value to capture — nobody falls to pick two — so a pick that takes the top player still on the board floors at A regardless of ADP. That floor is suspended when the sources are split, because it is computed from VORP, and VORP has no concept of a player being unable to play.

Roster need moves a grade one step. Filling an empty starting slot earns it; adding depth while a starting slot sits empty loses it.

Grades measure decision quality against what was knowable on the night. Projection R² is about 0.24 — outcome noise is roughly twice the spread of the projections — so none of this predicts the season.
