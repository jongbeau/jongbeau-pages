Get ready for Green Wave

Green Wave plays power football: multiple tight ends, fullbacks, and a quarterback under center—no shotgun. They try to run behind extra blockers, then mix in tosses and bootlegs to the wide side of the field. Our first priority: stop the run. They rarely pass, and the passes in this film are short. Play run first—don’t let worry about the pass make you hesitate.

1. Disrupt their power running game

In their main formation, two backs line up behind the quarterback. Extra tight ends line up on one side of the line. That is the strong side in this look.

They often run toward those extra blockers. They occasionally run the other way.

Your jobDefensive line: get off the ball quickly and penetrate your assigned gap—the space you defend. Quick penetration can disrupt the quarterback and handoff before the play develops. Find the strong side and keep your assignment.

Linebackers: play run first. Read your keys, step up into your assigned gap, and pursue the ball. Their passes are usually short; don't worry about getting receivers running downfield. Stop the run and we win.

Before playing the clip: where are the extra tight ends?

See the space

2. Watch the wide side of the field

The hash marks are the short white marks inside the field. When the ball is near one hash, there is more room on the opposite side.

Green Wave complements the power run with tosses and QB bootlegs into that extra space. It is an alert, not a guarantee.

Your jobIf you are the outside defender, keep the runner from getting around you. Everyone else: follow your assignment and pursue the ball.
See who has the ball

3. Watch for the bootleg

A bootleg is when the quarterback fakes a handoff to the halfback (HB), keeps the ball, and runs the opposite way himself.

Do not decide who has the ball just because the back runs past you.

Your jobIf you are assigned to the quarterback, stay responsible for him after the fake. Everyone else keeps their own job.

Run first. React to the short pass. They do not pass much, so don’t hang back expecting a deep throw. Read your keys and attack when you see run. If your keys show pass, take your assigned receiver or zone.

After watching: who had the ball, and which way did he go?

Extra-point alert: be ready for the short pass. On 2 of 3 charted XPs, they lined up with three receivers left and rolled the QB left to throw short. Both passes were incomplete. The other XP was a jet sweep. After a touchdown, recognize the formation, keep the QB contained, and stay with your assigned receiver.

Only three examples from the Texans film (plays 22, 30 and 68). A pass alert, not a guarantee.

Can you teach it back?

Say your answer to a teammate before opening it.

What do we play first: run or pass?

Run first. Read your keys and be ready to stop the runner. Their occasional short passes do not change our first priority. Keep your assigned QB or coverage job.

Where do you look to find the strong side in their main formation?

Find the extra tight ends beside the line. Know our strength call, then play your assigned gap.

The ball is on the right hash. Where is the extra room?

To the left. Be ready for a wide run left, but keep your assignment because they can still run inside or pass.

What is a bootleg, and who stays responsible for the QB?

The QB fakes to the HB, then keeps the ball and runs the other way. The defender assigned to the QB stays responsible for him.

Play run first. Know your job. Find the ball. Pursue together.
