Opponent offense · Aggies vs Texans film study

Stop the QB run. Win the strong side.

This game will be smash mouth WAR between the undefeated teams. The Aggies offense is a simple, physical shotgun running game and they win by being more physical than the defense. They motion a wingback to the strong side, the halfback lead blocks, and the quarterback runs behind all the extra blockers. In the game vs the Texans they were ineffective in the first half, and they answered by coming out in the second-half with an extra tight end overloading the strong side and pounded the ball to that side scoring 3 TDs in the second half. Expect forceful blocking and a runner who keeps fighting through contact.

Three things to remember

1. Find the strong side

Find the tight end, then watch the wingback’s motion. Call out where the extra blockers finish. Strong side means the side with more blocking help. That is their favorite run side.

2. The QB is the runner

The HB often leads the blocking. Find the QB behind him. Iso goes between the guard and tackle. Off tackle goes just outside the tackle or tight end.

3. Be Physical

The Aggies are not that big, but they play fast and physical. Their blockers routinely pancake defenders. Griffin Ealand #12 has speed and power. Arm tackles will not be enough. Wrap low with the tackling technique and we must gang tackle him. We must fight through blockers and keep pursuing until the whistle.

Their halftime adjustment: More Physicality

They trailed 0–6 at halftime, after Play 37, with no big first-half plays in Mr. Wang’s review. They replaced one of their two WRs with a TE, kept both wingbacks, and repeatedly attacked off tackle toward the extra blockers.

Who gets the ball?

They line up two backs next to each other in the shotgun. Either one can be the QB on that play, depending on which back the center snaps the ball to. From the offense’s point of view, a snap to the left back usually means a run to the right; a snap to the right back usually means a run to the left. The other back becomes the lead blocker. Watch who receives the snap, then find the blocker leading him.

Our defensive jobs

Defensive line

Disrupt the backfield! Either push your blocker backwards, or shoot the strong side gap. If you can hit the lead blocker before he builds momentum the whole play falls apart. Do not get washed sideways and open a running lane.

Linebackers

Follow the motion to shift our defense to the strong side. Read the lead blocker, fight through blockers and get to the ball. Must gang tackle.

Edge defenders

Keep outside leverage under our defensive call. Make the runner turn back toward help. Do not chase motion inside and give away the corner.

Secondary

Support the run quickly once you read it, while keeping your assigned receiver. They threw short to #7 and tried a deep pass. If the QB scrambles, rally without abandoning coverage early.

Teach it with three clips

Watch how hard #12 runs on Plays 47 and 73. Then watch Play 57: the same heavy look can produce a short touchdown pass.

Quick player check

The wingback moves across. What do we call out?

The new strong side—the side with the extra blockers. Keep our assigned gaps.

The HB runs ahead. Who are we looking for?

The QB behind the lead blocker. This is a QB-run offense.

#12 breaks the first tackle. What happens next?

Everyone keeps pursuing. Wrap and finish together.
